## Description

<!-- Please only use this template for submitting enhancement requests -->

**What would you like to be added**:

Add **time-window-based capacity reservation** to Kueue: a declarative mechanism to reserve (hold) a specified amount of resources for a **future recurring time window** (e.g. daily EOD batch window), ensuring the reserved capacity is **not allocated to other workloads** as the window approaches and during the window.  

A proposed new API (name TBD, e.g. `ResourceReservation`) would allow cluster admins to:
- Define a time window (`start`, `end`, `timeZone`) and recurrence (daily/weekly/cron-like).
- Specify resources to reserve (CPU/memory/GPU, optionally flavors).
- Configure a lead time (`preemptBefore`) to proactively reclaim capacity before the window opens.
- Optionally allow borrowing outside the window (`borrowBeforeWindow`) while guaranteeing reclaim by window start.
- Configure reclaim behavior (e.g. preempt lower-priority workloads to satisfy the reservation).

**Why is this needed**:

Kueue currently doesn’t provide **guaranteed advance reservation** for a future window. This is critical in finance and other SLA-driven environments where batch workloads (e.g. end-of-day risk calculations / regulatory reporting) must start and complete within strict time windows.  

Current workarounds (CronJob + high priority ClusterQueue + preemption) are insufficient:
- **No guarantee semantics**: capacity reclaim at window start is best-effort and can be incomplete under contention.
- **No proactive reclaim/scaling**: the system has no “future slot” concept to prepare capacity ahead of time.
- **Non-atomic readiness**: preemption/reclaim happens incrementally and may not converge before the deadline.

A first-class reservation window would provide predictable, auditable behavior and reduce operational burden.

### Comment by [@amy](https://github.com/amy) — 2026-05-27T16:46:05Z

@gyliu513 I have a similar worry. What would be helpful is...
1. having a concrete end-user that would use this to guide your design 
2. whatever design you come up with, to bring it up to wg-batch meeting to discuss with maintainers there

The reason why this is complicated is:
- you need to figure out what primitives are missing at what layer. For instance, some of these primitives could be missing at the k8s scheduler layer. 
- you may or may not need a db that holds predictive runtimes of workloads
- you may or may not need a backfill scheduler 
- Kueue and for instance Slurm have fundamentally different scheduling concepts. For instance, lets say you have a low priority cron. Its existing scheduling queue is already filled. It cannot compete for burst. Would your semantic then increase priority of that low priority cron to kick out other workloads in that CQ? 

Overall, I do want something in this area, but not at the cost of increased complexity where its confusing why something is pending/preempted. And want something that directly maps to an end-user.
