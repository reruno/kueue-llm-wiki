### Comment by [@TapanManu](https://github.com/TapanManu) — 2026-06-16T10:49:17Z

> Basically, I would like to understand first what was the reason for the container restart, if we have this information. Was it maybe due to failing leader election requests on timeouts?

### Anatomy of the CI Flake
Here is exactly how the race condition plays out in the runner based on your log timeline:

22:14:06.643 – Ginkgo starts checking the management cluster. The deployment is technically marked "Available" by Kubernetes.

22:14:06.753 – Kueue tries to acquire its leader election lease (c1f6bfd2.kueue.x-k8s.io).

The Bottleneck – Because 5 parallel processes and other ML operators are hammering the API server, the background network driver delays the packet transmission. The cert-controller inside Kueue misses its startup window, throws the i/o timeout we saw in your local logs, and crashes.

22:14:07.060 – A mere 0.4 seconds later, Ginkgo checks the restart counter again. It notices the container restarted exactly 1 time due to that cert panic, pulls the emergency brake, and kills the entire test run.

This is what i have observed and timestamped @mimowo
