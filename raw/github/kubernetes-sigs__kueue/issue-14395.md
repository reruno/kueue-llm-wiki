## Description

### What happened:

A prebuilt Workload that stops matching its Job is finished for reason `OutOfSync` before anything stops the Job. If that Workload was admitted and its Job is running, its quota is released while its Pods keep running.

The order, on `main`:

1. `pkg/controller/jobframework/reconciler.go:423`, step 1, calls `ensureOneWorkload`.
2. `reconciler.go:1018-1020`: a prebuilt Workload that fails the comparison returns `(nil, nil)`, so the reconcile continues as though the Job had no Workload.
3. `reconciler.go:1370-1377`: before returning that, `ensurePrebuiltWorkloadInSync` calls `workloadfinish.Finish(ctx, r.client, wl, kueue.WorkloadFinishedReasonOutOfSync, msg, r.clock)`. There is no `job.IsActive()` check and no `stopJob` ahead of it.
4. `pkg/controller/core/workload_controller.go:1339` takes the `StatusFinished` branch and `:1357` calls `r.cache.DeleteWorkload`. The quota goes back to the ClusterQueue.
5. Only now, at `reconciler.go:520`, `wl == nil` sends the Job to `handleJobWithNoWorkload`, which at `:1793` calls `r.stopJob(ctx, job, nil, StopReasonNoMatchingWorkload, "missing workload")`. The `nil` is the Workload the PodSetInfo would have been restored from.
6. `reconciler.go:1802`: if the Job is still active, the reconcile logs `Job is suspended but still has active pods, waiting` and returns. The Pods run on against quota the ClusterQueue has already handed to someone else.

A second, milder face of the same order: step 3, `handle job is finished` at `reconciler.go:496`, only sets `Succeeded` or `Failed` when `wl != nil`. A completed Job whose prebuilt Workload failed the comparison keeps `OutOfSync`, since step 1 already returned nil for it.

### What you expected to happen:

The Job stops, and its quota is held until it has no active Pods, before the Workload it was admitted under is finished.

### How to reproduce it (as minimally and precisely as possible):

I could not, through the API, and I think that is the useful part of this report rather than a gap in it.

Reaching the harmful state needs an admitted Workload and a running Job that stop matching. Both sides are frozen by then. `pkg/webhooks/workload_webhook.go:366-368` runs `validateImmutablePodSets` once `HasQuotaReservation` is true, and `validateImmutablePodSet` at `:465-470` ends in `apivalidation.ValidateImmutableField` over the whole `PodSet`, template included. A Job's `spec.template` is immutable to the apiserver. Calling `ValidateWorkloadUpdate` directly with a changed `runtimeClassName` gives:

```
no quota reservation     allowed
with quota reservation   spec.podSets.0: Invalid value: {...}: field is immutable
```

So neither object can move into a mismatch while the Job runs. What can move is the comparison. Anything that adds a field to `comparePodTemplate` makes a pair that was equivalent under the old binary non-equivalent under the new one, with neither object touched, and an admitted Workload with a running Job meets the new comparison on the first reconcile after the restart.

That has not happened yet: `git log -S comparePodTemplate` finds only `c03612ea9`, the move into the framework. #14289 would be the first, adding `runtimeClassName`, which is how I came to look at this. The order above is older than that PR and independent of it, so I am reporting it separately rather than folding a jobframework state-machine change into an accounting fix.

### Anything else we need to know?:

`stopJob` with a nil Workload is a second thing worth deciding on rather than only the ordering. Where a Job is stopped for eviction it gets the Workload, and `RestorePodSetsInfo` puts back what the admission changed. The prebuilt no-match path has never had one to give.

### Comment by [@thc1006](https://github.com/thc1006) — 2026-08-13T13:53:40Z

Prior art worth linking, since it shows this failure shape has already been accepted as a bug once.

#13046 was the MultiKueue side of it: a remote Workload finished `OutOfSync` had that condition mirrored onto the manager Workload, and the manager Job was left stranded. #13047 fixed it by retrying instead of copying the finish across.

The path here is the local one, where the finish is decided in `ensurePrebuiltWorkloadInSync` rather than copied from a remote, so it is a different call site rather than a duplicate. The consequence is the same class though: a Workload finished because it stopped matching takes its quota and its place in the cache with it, while the Job it was admitted for is still running.
