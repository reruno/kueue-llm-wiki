## Description

**What would you like to be added**:
  Classify the preemptor ClusterQueue's quota position before building the preemption candidate iterator, and skip collecting and sorting candidate pools that provably cannot yield a valid preemption:
1. **Preemptor exceeds its borrowing limit**: 
when the preemptor CQ's usage + new workload request is already at or above its borrowing limit, preempting workloads in other queues cannot make room (the preemptor cannot borrow at all). Only the same-queue candidate pool is relevant; hierarchy and priority candidate pools should not be collected or sorted.
  2. **No candidates from other queues / borrowing within cohort forbidden**:
   the conditions already evaluated at`pkg/scheduler/preemption/preemption.go` (the `NoCandidateFromOtherQueues || (borrowWithinCohortForbidden && ...)` short-circuit) should be evaluated *before* candidate collection, not after.

Today `NewCandidateIterator` (`pkg/scheduler/preemption/classical/candidate_generator.go`) unconditionally collects and sorts all three candidate pools (same-queue, hierarchy, priority) on every preemption simulation, and the caller may discard the result without iterating a single candidate.This is a behavior-preserving performance optimization: the skipped pools are exactly those the existing short-circuit logic would discard.

**Why is this needed**:
Candidate collection is O(number of workloads in the cohort subtree) and each pool is sorted (superlinear). In a large production cluster with ~10k pending workloads, a few ClusterQueues operating near their borrowing limits triggered full candidate collection and sorting on every scheduling cycle. Observed impact:
- Workload admission throughput dropped from 30-40/s to 2-4/s.
- Flame graphs (captured with fair sharing both enabled and disabled) show candidate collection and sorting (`NewCandidateIterator`, `collectCandidatesForHierarchicalReclaim`, `slices.SortFunc`) as the hottest frames in the scheduling loop.
