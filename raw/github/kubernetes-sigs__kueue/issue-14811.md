## Description

# What happened:

A Kueue-managed JobSet was admitted and running normally for ~20h. A single child Job restart triggered the JobSet's failurePolicy (restartStrategy: Recreate), which recreated all child Jobs. During that in-flight recreate, the full pod set did not simultaneously reach readiness within the waitForPodsReady window, and Kueue evicted the Workload via the recovery path: `status.schedulingStats.evictions[].underlyingCause: WaitForRecovery`, condition reason `PodsReadyTimeout`.

Kueue then stopped the Workload: all pods were killed and deleted, and the JobSet was suspended (spec.suspend: true). However, the Workload object was left in a contradictory terminal state and its quota was never released:

- `Admitted=True`, `QuotaReserved=True`, and `Evicted=True` are all present at once.
- `.status.admission` remains fully populated (flavour assignments + resource usage).
- `status.requeueState.requeueAt` passed (only count: 1, well under backoffLimitCount + 1), but the Workload was never requeued or re-admitted.
- No further controller reconciles occurred for this Workload after the suspend.
- The ClusterQueue kept counting this Workload's GPU reservation for hours with zero running pods, blocking admission of other workloads borrowing the same flavour in the cohort.

Net effect: a "phantom" quota reservation. The Workload holds admission accounting indefinitely while the JobSet is suspended and no pods exist. It does not self-heal.

# What you expected to happen:

On a waitForPodsReady recovery-timeout eviction, Kueue should either:

1. Clear `.status.admission`, set `Admitted=False`/`QuotaReserved=False`, release the ClusterQueue reservation, and requeue per `requeuingStrategy` (backoff) or deactivate on limit; or
2. If the intent is to hold admission across a JobSet restart, keep the Job/pods active — not tear them down and suspend the JobSet while still counting quota.

The observed state is neither: pods are gone, the JobSet is suspended, yet the reservation persists and the Workload is stuck (not requeued, not deactivated). At minimum, `Evicted=True` should never coexist with `Admitted=True`/`QuotaReserved=True` and a populated `.status.admission`.

# How to reproduce it (as minimally and precisely as possible):

Not yet reduced to a guaranteed-minimal repro, but the triggering conditions appear to be:

1. Kueue with `waitForPodsReady` enabled (timeout, `requeuingStrategy.backoffLimitCount`) and the JobSet integration enabled.
2. A JobSet with `failurePolicy.restartStrategy: Recreate` (and a non-trivial maxRestarts).
3. An admitted, running Workload whose JobSet performs a JobSet-level Recreate restart (all child Jobs recreated), e.g. one child Job fails/completes and triggers recreate.
4. Pods stay non-ready long enough during that recreate (e.g. a slow reschedule / brief FailedScheduling) that the recovery path fires the PodsReady timeout while the Recreate is still in flight.
5. Observe: the recovery eviction races the JobSet suspend/teardown. The Workload ends with `Admitted=QuotaReserved=Evicted=true`, `.status.admission` populated, `requeueState.requeueAt` in the past, no subsequent reconcile, and the ClusterQueue reservation not released.

Metric evidence: the ClusterQueue reservation gauge for the flavour (`kueue_cluster_queue_resource_reservation{...}`) stayed flat across the pod-teardown/suspend timestamp and never decreased by this Workload's share. Its GPUs continued to be counted against the ClusterQueue for many hours while zero pods for the JobSet existed — confirming the leak is in accounting, not just a stale status field.

Config note: this deployment sets `waitForPodsReady.timeout: 30m` and `requeuingStrategy.backoffLimitCount: 1`, and does not set `recoveryTimeout` explicitly, yet the eviction was recorded with `underlyingCause: WaitForRecovery`. The affected flavour has `nominalQuota: 0` with unlimited cohort borrowing (GPU capacity is borrowed cohort-wide), so a stuck reservation directly subtracts from other tenants' borrowable capacity.

# Question for maintainers

Is the recovery-eviction path expected to run when `recoveryTimeout` is unset, and should eviction always clear `.status.admission` / release quota even when the underlying Job is concurrently being suspended by the JobSet controller (Recreate restart in flight)? The race between JobSet `Recreate` teardown and the Kueue recovery-timeout eviction looks central.

**2. Kueue's last log line for `<workload>` around the stop**

Neither `Updating the PodsReady` condition nor `No update for PodsReady condition` — for two reasons, the second being the interesting one:

- We run kueue v0.19.0 at V(2); both messages are `log.V(3)` (reconciler.go:530/554), so they'd be invisible even on a healthy path (confirmed: neither string appears for any workload cluster-wide in our logs).
- More importantly, the reconciler never reached that code. From T-42m onward, **every** reconcile of this JobSet aborted at step 4 (reclaimable pods) with an error that is visible at any verbosity:

```
    Updating reclaimable pods
  error: admission webhook "[vworkload.kb.io](http://vworkload.kb.io/)" denied the request:
         status.reclaimablePods[worker]: Required value: cannot be removed
  at [sigs.k8s.io/kueue/pkg/controller/jobframework.(*JobReconciler).ReconcileGenericJob](http://sigs.k8s.io/kueue/pkg/controller/jobframework.(*JobReconciler).ReconcileGenericJob)
     pkg/controller/jobframework/reconciler.go:515
```

Mechanism: a succeeded child Job contributes reclaimable pods for podset `worker`; when the completed Job is recreated (fresh, not finished), the computed entry drops to 0 and `UpdateReclaimablePods` tries to remove it — which kueue's own Workload validating webhook rejects. `ReconcileGenericJob` returns the error, so steps 5+ (waitForPodsReady handling, eviction handling, requeue/finish) never execute. Retries hit the identical rejection forever; we observed the loop on capped backoff from T-42m until the JobSet was deleted at T+4h52m (last occurrence T+4h36m).

The exact last lines for `<workload>` around the stop:
- workload-reconciler: T+4m `Workload update event` (status still `admitted`) → `Reconcile Workload` → event `Stopped` ("Exceeded the PodsReady timeout") — then **total silence** for this workload until its deletion at T+4h52m.
[10:33 AM]- jobframework (`jobset` logger): the `Updating reclaimable pods` webhook denial + `Reconciler error`, immediately after the stop and on every retry thereafter.

Two observations that may be useful:

1. The 4-minute gap between eviction (T+0) and the stop (T+4m) looks like the same deadlock: reconciles at T+0, T+1.4m and T+3m59s all died at step 4. At T+4m `worker-3` happened to complete again, transiently making the computed reclaimablePods equal to the stored value, so exactly one reconcile passed step 4 and reached eviction handling → the suspend went through. Suspension then recreated the child Job, the succeeded count flapped back, and every later reconcile failed at step 4 again — so the evicted workload was never requeued/finished and held its quota for ~4h50m. It was only released when we deleted the JobSet and kueue logged `Workload is orphaned; finishing to release quota`.

2. So the phantom reservation generalizes: any condition that makes the jobframework reconciler persistently error before the requeue/finish steps (here, a webhook-rejected reclaimablePods removal caused by completed child Jobs being recreated) strands an evicted workload with quota reserved indefinitely.
