**What happened**:

With `ElasticJobsViaWorkloadSlices`, a scale-up replacement can cause a scheduling cycle to commit more quota than the cohort has.

The accounting disagrees in two places:

- the replacement is charged only for its increase over the replaced slice;
- the replaced slice is also in the cycle-wide `preemptedWorkloads` set, so `fits()` simulates releasing its full usage.

As a result, the replaced slice's quota is effectively released twice during fit checks.

**What you expected to happen**:

A scale-up replacement should consume the same net quota as an equivalent non-elastic workload and must not let the scheduling cycle commit above the ClusterQueue or cohort capacity.

**How to reproduce it (as minimally and precisely as possible)**:

Scheduler unit level. `FairSharingRefill` is disabled.

Two ClusterQueues share a cohort with 10 CPU:

- `cq-a` already has elastic slice `old` admitted for 6 CPU;
- `grow` replaces `old`, scaling 6 → 8 CPU (net +2);
- `cq-b` has an ordinary 4 CPU workload `b1`.

The ClusterQueue split determines the scheduling order:

| Case | Cohort split | Order | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| later entry observes the bad state | `cq-a` 10, `cq-b` 0 (borrowing limit 10) | grow → b1 | both admitted: 12 / 10 CPU |
| replacement's own fit is permissive | `cq-a` 6, `cq-b` 4 | b1 → grow | both admitted: 12 / 10 CPU |

Control: replace `grow` with an ordinary 2 CPU workload, keeping the same net increase. The scheduler correctly rejects the workload that no longer fits and stays within the 10 CPU cohort.

`old` is then finished with reason `WorkloadSliceReplaced`, leaving `grow` (8 CPU) and `b1` (4 CPU), so the 12/10 result is a real overcommit rather than a transient accounting artifact.

**Anything else we need to know?**:

- The same accounting path is present on `release-0.18` and `release-0.19`.
- This is different from #12538 / #13185: it reproduces with a single replacement target in `Fit`, without preemption.
- There is a related TAS accounting issue, #TAS, where the replaced slice remains counted in the topology snapshot instead.
