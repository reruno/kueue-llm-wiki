## Description

**Why is this needed**:

Whole-workload preemption is especially disruptive for Spark:

- **No checkpointing.** A Spark application's progress (DAG, completed stages, map-output locations)
  lives in the driver. Evicting the Workload suspends the application and kills the driver, so it
  restarts from scratch. A 6-hour job preempted at 90% completion reruns in full, and ~5.4 hours of
  compute is wasted.
- **Spark can survive losing executors.** Spark already tolerates executor loss by retrying tasks and
  recomputing lost shuffle. Graceful decommissioning
  ([SPARK-20629](https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/SPARK-20629): `spark.decommission.enabled`,
  `spark.storage.decommission.enabled`, `spark.storage.decommission.shuffleBlocks.enabled`,
  `spark.storage.decommission.rddBlocks.enabled`) makes this cheap: a terminating executor stops
  taking tasks and migrates its shuffle and cached RDD blocks to peer executors within the Pod's
  `terminationGracePeriodSeconds`, so the job continues without recomputation.

The preemptor needs only quota, and freeing part of a Spark job's executors provides it at a small
cost to the job. Kueue instead throws away the whole application.

**Why Kueue preempts whole workloads today**:

Preemption ([KEP-83](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/kueue/tree/main/keps/83-workload-preemption),
[docs](https://kueue.sigs.k8s.io/docs/concepts/preemption/)) exists so that quota can be recovered
quickly instead of waiting an unbounded time for workloads to finish. It is triggered by:

- **Priority-aware scheduling**: a higher-priority Workload preempts lower-priority ones in the same
  ClusterQueue (`withinClusterQueue`).
- **Quota reclamation**: a ClusterQueue under its nominal quota takes back quota lent to borrowing
  ClusterQueues in its cohort (`reclaimWithinCohort`), optionally also while it is itself
  borrowing (`borrowWithinCohort`).
- **Fair Sharing**: Workloads from the ClusterQueues with the highest weighted share are preempted
  until the preemptor's ClusterQueue reaches its fair share
  (`LessThanOrEqualToFinalShare`, `LessThanInitialShare`).

The *unit* of preemption is the whole Workload, for structural reasons:

- **The Workload is the unit of admission.** Kueue admits with all-or-nothing (gang) semantics and
  accounts quota per Workload, so candidate simulation adds or removes whole Workloads from the
  snapshot.
- **Kueue's control over a job is `suspend`.** Integrations stop a job through its suspend field,
  which tears down all of its Pods. Kueue does not own the Pods and cannot shrink a job on its own.
- **Partial preemption was an explicit non-goal** of KEP-83, and graceful termination was left to
  the workload.
- **Candidate selection is greedy.** Candidates are sorted (borrowing queues first, then lowest
  priority, then most recently admitted), then taken in order until the preemptor fits, and the
  resulting set is minimised in reverse. This keeps the number of preempted *Workloads* small, but
  each victim loses all of its Pods. The Fair Sharing docs already call out the "tolerance to
  disruptions, in particular when single Workloads use a significant amount of the borrowable
  resources."

These semantics suit gang-scheduled training jobs. They are overly aggressive for elastic workloads
like Spark, where a partial reduction is enough.

**What would you like to be added**:

Spark-aware partial preemption that reclaims **executors**, not whole applications. It keeps the
existing triggers (priority, reclamation, Fair Sharing) and changes only what happens to the victim:

1. **The driver should never be preempted.** The driver is a single Pod that holds all of the
   application's state, so it is treated as premium: it goes away only through whole-workload
   eviction. Only the executor PodSet is reducible.
2. **A subset of executors stays running.** A **configurable maximum reclaim percentage** caps how
   much of a workload's admitted executors can be reclaimed in total across preemption rounds. It is
   set per workload by an annotation, with a default on the ClusterQueue or in the Kueue
   configuration. The remaining executors also serve as migration targets for decommissioned
   executors' blocks. Once a workload hits its cap, it is only eligible for whole-workload eviction.
3. **Algorithm-driven victim selection, not greedy.** Instead of draining the first candidate in sort
   order, spread the reduction across as many eligible Spark workloads as possible within the lowest
   eligible priority tier, minimising the largest share any single application loses. Priority and
   Fair Sharing ordering still decide *which tier* is touched first.
4. **Driver-side executor selection.** Kueue decides *how many* executors to reclaim from *which*
   workload. The Spark driver, which already knows each executor's running tasks and shuffle/cached
   data, decides *which* ones, preferring idle executors and those holding the least data. As an
   option, the driver can publish its ranking in the standard `controller.kubernetes.io/pod-deletion-cost`
   Pod annotation, so whichever component removes Pods follows the driver's choice without reading
   Spark metrics.

**Considerations**:

- **Driver-aware reclaim and grow-back.** With a fixed executor count, the driver treats
  `spark.executor.instances` as its target and replaces any executor that disappears. So the driver
  must lower its own target when executors are reclaimed (ideally by removing them itself, per
  item 4). Otherwise it keeps replacement Pods parked, waiting for quota. When quota becomes
  available, Kueue raises the admitted count and the driver raises its target back, never above
  `spark.executor.instances`. Grow-back should be ordered by the workload's priority and original
  admission time, so a shrunk job regains executors before newer jobs of equal priority take the
  capacity.
- **Quota safety.** Quota is released only after the executors are gone (as in KEP-975's two-phase
  model). The preemptor's wait is bounded by `terminationGracePeriodSeconds`.

### Comment by [@zhengchenyu](https://github.com/zhengchenyu) — 2026-09-29T08:40:16Z

@mimowo 
There are three main reasons for us:

* Workload slicing generates a large volume of workloads; we internally estimate that at least tens of thousands of Spark jobs will migrate to Kueue, and dynamic scaling would result in an excessive number of workloads.
* We currently prefer for Spark to directly control the workload, whereas workload slicing makes it difficult for Spark to track the workloads.
* I think the combination of workload resizing and partial admission is better.

I have posted the documentation in the [Meeting Docs](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1XOeUN-K0aKmJJNq7H07r74n-mGgSFyiEDQ3ecwsGhec/edit?tab=t.0); for specific details, please refer to: [workloadresize](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1V0QwZee0z5ROOC9MA-N5pjrc57Z9PNsdMcGnp2AxwY8/edit?tab=t.0#heading=h.ofka7zbipfb2)
