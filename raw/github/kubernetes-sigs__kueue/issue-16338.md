## Description

**What would you like to be added**:

The core idea is to sort once and reuse within the scheduling cycle, and avoid unnecessary sorting:

1. Introduce a per-CQ candidate-ordering cache (CandidateOrderCache). Scoped to the scheduling cycle (Snapshot), it caches each ClusterQueue's sorted workloads and the length of its evicted prefix. The entry is safe to reuse across preemptors: the preemptor-dependent inputs to the ordering are equal for any two workloads of the same CQ, so they do not affect the intra-CQ order. Entries are invalidated and rebuilt when a CQ's membership changes, and can be warmed concurrently.
2. Produce candidates lazily. Candidates are organized into per-CQ buckets across the fixed 6 segments (evicted / non-evicted × hierarchy / priority / same-queue) and yielded on demand via a k-way merge, paying only for the candidates actually consumed rather than sorting the full set up front.

> Note: I think "fair preempt" can also be optimized in a similar way. But Let's focus our efforts on optimizing "classical preempt" first.

**Why is this needed**:

At scale, the preemption phase adds noticeable scheduling latency. Flame graphs point to candidate workload sorting as the hotspot.

The root cause is that classical preemption's candidate generation re-sorts the same workloads repeatedly:

- Every preemptor and every flavor probe runs a full slices.SortFunc over the candidate workloads, with no caching.
- Within a single scheduling cycle, the candidates of a given ClusterQueue are re-sorted over and over, even though the ordering is stable.
- The sort covers all of a CQ's candidates, but only a small fraction is ever consumed, so most of the sorting work is wasted.

# Results

> Note: This is a stress scenario designed specifically for large-scale preemption; real-world sheduling performance will be better.

## Experiment Setup

The cluster topology has a two-level structure: a root cohort with two child cohorts, parent-a and parent-b, each containing 10 ClusterQueues. Every queue has a nominal quota of 50 and has ReclaimWithinCohort: Any and BorrowWithinCohort: LowerPriority enabled.

The experiment runs in two phases:

1. At t=90s, parent-a's 10 queues each submit 500 workloads (5,000 total), each requesting 200m CPU and running for 300 seconds. These workloads gradually borrow quota from parent-b.
2. At t=180s, parent-b's 10 queues each submit 500 workloads, triggering parent-b to reclaim its quota from parent-a by preempting the workloads that were borrowing it.

This is a pure reclaim scenario designed to measure preemption throughput and scheduling latency at scale — 20 ClusterQueues and up to 10,000 workloads in flight simultaneously.

We can reproduce the experiment with #16463 and [reclaim.yaml](https://github.com/user-attachments/files/32884810/reclaim.yaml)

## Results

I used kueue-performance-scheduler to run a simulated test. Initially, I observed a significant throughput drop in preemption-heavy workloads on our v0.14.5, where scheduling throughput fell to just 0.4 ops/s.

The community has already put significant effort into reducing redundant preemptor scheduling cycles. When I tested v0.20-rc.1, throughput improved to 18–36 ops/s.

With the candidate ordering cache introduced in this PR, throughput further improved to 104–117 ops/s. The `schedule` execution time dropped substantially as well, and flame graphs confirm that sorting no longer dominates the CPU profile.
