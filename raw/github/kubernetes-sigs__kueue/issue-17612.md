**What would you like to be added**:

A Fair Sharing mode where the share (DRS) used to compare two subtrees is computed only over the flavor-resources (FRs) requested by the workload being considered, and is used the same way in admission ordering and in preemption. "Within nominal" then simply means `share == 0`, so `FairSharingPrioritizeNonBorrowing` and `FairSharingPreemptWithinNominal` are no longer needed.

Today [`dominantResourceShare`](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/kueue/blob/main/pkg/cache/scheduler/fair_sharing.go) sums, for each resource, the usage above nominal over *all* flavors, divides it by the parent's lendable, and takes the maximum over *all* resources. Borrowing on a flavor the workload doesn't use therefore raises its share. This led to two boolean checks that bypass DRS (#9406):
- `FairSharingPrioritizeNonBorrowing` (#9484, #10149): the admission tournament prefers a subtree that doesn't borrow on the workload's requested FRs, before comparing DRS.
- `FairSharingPreemptWithinNominal` (#9494, #17034): preemption skips the DRS strategies when the preemptor's ClusterQueue doesn't borrow on the requested FRs. #13307 extends this to Cohorts (#9466).

Proposal, behind a new alpha feature gate (e.g. `FairSharingWorkloadScopedShare`):
1. For a node `N` (ClusterQueue or Cohort) with parent `P`, with the workload `w` simulated:
   ```
   share_w(N) = max over fr in FRs(w) of  max(0, usage_N(fr) - quota_N(fr)) / lendable_P(fr),  divided by weight_N
   ```
   `FRs(w)` are the FRs with a non-zero request in `w`'s flavor assignment from nomination (for an admitted target, in its admission), which is what `FairSharingPrioritizeNonBorrowing` and `requestedFlavorResources` already use. It is neither every flavor `w` could match (borrowing on a matching but unassigned flavor would penalize it again, as in #9406, and admitting `w` wouldn't raise the share), nor only the FRs needing preemption (#17022).
2. Admission: the tournament compares `share_w(child)` at every level. `computeDRS` already computes a value per workload, so only the FR scope changes, and the non-borrowing step goes away.
3. Preemption: the strategies compare `share_P` of the preemptor's AlmostLCA (with `P`) against `share_T` of the target's AlmostLCA (with and without `T`). Each workload is measured on its own FRs, i.e. exactly the value the tournament would use for it. `FairSharingVerifyFinalTargets` uses the same function, and the target ordering keeps scoping to the contested FRs (#16738).
4. Flavor assignment: `PreemptionOracle.SimulatePreemption` runs the strategies for one candidate FR at a time (`workloadUsage = {fr: quantity}`), before the assignment is complete. Since `share_w` is a max of per-FR terms that depend only on that FR, the oracle computes exactly the term of the candidate flavor, so flavors can be compared by the share they would add. That term is a lower bound of the final share. The oracle can therefore be optimistic for multi-FR workloads (the final preemption with the full assignment finds no targets, costing a cycle), but it cannot make preemption and admission disagree. With today's aggregate share, the candidate flavor's term is mixed with borrowing on all other flavors and resources, so it can be dominated by borrowing the choice doesn't affect.
5. Reclaiming nominal quota becomes a regular case of the strategies: `share_P == 0`, so `0 <= targetNewShare` (`LessThanOrEqualToFinalShare`) always holds, and `0 < targetOldShare` (`LessThanInitialShare`) holds for every target that borrows on its FRs. The `InCohortReclamation` reason can be derived from `share_P == 0`.

The trade-off is that classic DRF across resources is lost for borrowers. Example: a Cohort lends 100 CPU and 8 GPU; `cq-a` and `cq-b` have no quota; `cq-a` borrows all 8 GPUs and `cq-b` borrows 20 CPU. Today a 10 CPU workload from `cq-a` has share `max(8/8, 10/100) = 1`, so it loses to `cq-b` (0.2) and cannot preempt it. With the proposal its share is `10/100 = 0.1`: it goes first and can preempt `cq-b` until their CPU shares even out. That's why I'd start with an opt-in mode.

Open questions:
- Should this replace the current share eventually, or stay selectable through the Configuration API?
- A middle ground keeps the aggregate DRS for borrowers and only maps "within nominal on FRs(w)" to 0. It keeps cross-resource DRF, but also keeps the gap in the proof's assumption (see below) when the dominant resource is one the workload doesn't use.
- Normalization per FR (as above), or per resource name summed over the requested flavors? `Lendable` is currently aggregated per resource name. Per-FR normalization keeps the per-FR terms independent, which item 4 relies on.
- Borrowing something with nothing lendable yields share 0 (#16529). It needs explicit handling, so that `share == 0` keeps meaning "within nominal".
- Status and metrics (`weightedShare`, `kueue_cluster_queue_weighted_share`, `kueue_cohort_weighted_share`) report a per-node value, while decisions would use per-workload values. Keep the aggregate value there and log the per-workload ones?
- The share is evaluated at the compared node (the child of the tournament level, the AlmostLCA in preemption). A ClusterQueue's own nominal quota that a sibling is using therefore doesn't count at higher levels (cf. #13320). This intentionally drops the ClusterQueue-level claim against distant targets that `FairSharingPreemptWithinNominal` grants today.
- The assignment belongs to a nomination, not to the workload. After issuing preemptions the flavor scan restarts from the first flavor, so with flavor fungibility the preemptor may get different flavors in the next cycle, and the share the tournament compares then differs from the one that justified the preemption. Usually it gets the same flavors, since its targets freed them. Should it stick to those flavors until its targets are gone?

**Why is this needed**:

The bypasses keep producing preemption ↔ re-admission loops, each fixed with another targeted check:
- #16665, #16666 (fixed by #16738): the target's DRS was raised by borrowing on an uncontested flavor, so preemption picked it, and the tournament, which only looks at requested FRs, re-admitted it ahead of the preemptor.
- #17022 (fixed by #17034): the preemptor was within nominal on the contested FRs, so preemption skipped DRS, but it borrowed on another requested FR, so the tournament didn't prefer it.
- Found while reviewing #13307: preemption accepts the nominal claim of a node below the AlmostLCA (an intermediate Cohort with #13307, the ClusterQueue on `main`), while the tournament at the LCA evaluates the AlmostLCA. The AlmostLCA is borrowing because a sibling uses that quota. In an integration test, the same workload is preempted 112 times in 8s with #13307, and 114 times on `main` when the quota sits on the ClusterQueue.

They all follow the same pattern: a boolean check that skips DRS has to agree exactly (same node, same FRs) across admission ordering, target ordering, preemption and final verification. Otherwise preemption evicts a workload that the tournament then re-admits ahead of the preemptor.

With a single workload-scoped share, the existing [proof that two workloads won't preempt each other](https://kueue.sigs.k8s.io/docs/concepts/fair_sharing/) applies without exceptions, and extends to re-admission:
- The proof assumes `DRS_pending < DRS_admitted`, which fails today when the workload doesn't use the dominant resource. When the share only covers the workload's own FRs, it holds for every subtree that borrows on them.
- No preemption skips the strategies, so Lemma 1 always holds: `A preempts B => share_A_admitted < share_B_admitted`. In the next cycle the tournament compares these same two values at the LCA, so it admits `A` before `B`.

It also covers #9466 without additional code, and would let us deprecate two Beta feature gates.
