**What happened**:

When a workload $W_1$ is processed earlier in the scheduling cycle and triggers preemption of victim workload(s) $V$:
1. [`Scheduler.processEntry`](file:///usr/local/google/home/dpastuszka/gitrepos/kueue/pkg/scheduler/scheduler.go#L578-L579) records $V$ in `preemptedWorkloads` and calls [`bookUsage`](file:///usr/local/google/home/dpastuszka/gitrepos/kueue/pkg/scheduler/scheduler.go#L772-L784), which **adds $W_1$'s quota and TAS domain usage** to `snapshot` **without removing $V$** from `snapshot`.
2. When a subsequent workload $W_2$ runs [`entry.checkFits`](file:///usr/local/google/home/dpastuszka/gitrepos/kueue/pkg/scheduler/scheduler.go#L763-L766), [`fits`](file:///usr/local/google/home/dpastuszka/gitrepos/kueue/pkg/scheduler/scheduler.go#L947-L953) temporarily strips `preemptedWorkloads` usage via [`Snapshot.SimulateWorkloadUsageRemoval`](file:///usr/local/google/home/dpastuszka/gitrepos/kueue/pkg/cache/scheduler/snapshot.go#L181-L201).
3. However, if $W_2$ fails the TAS check (`FitsCheckNoTAS`) and enters `needsTASRecompute` in [`Scheduler.updateAssignmentIfNeeded`](file:///usr/local/google/home/dpastuszka/gitrepos/kueue/pkg/scheduler/scheduler.go#L869-L945) **without** calling [`simulateOtherPreemptions`](file:///usr/local/google/home/dpastuszka/gitrepos/kueue/pkg/scheduler/scheduler.go#L1010-L1018), [`Scheduler.getAssignments`](file:///usr/local/google/home/dpastuszka/gitrepos/kueue/pkg/scheduler/scheduler.go#L1560-L1634) runs against a corrupt snapshot state that simultaneously holds **both the incoming preemptor $W_1$'s booked usage and the evicted victim $V$'s usage, pods, and workload entry**.

**What you expected to happen**:

Before running `Scheduler.getAssignments` during `needsTASRecompute` (and throughout native TAS assignment and preemption target selection), workloads already in `preemptedWorkloads` for the current scheduling cycle should have their quota usage, TAS usage, `cq.Workloads` entries, and simulator pods excluded from the snapshot (via `simulateOtherPreemptions`), and workloads that fit only because of those in-flight preemptions should be handled as `DeferredFit` rather than re-preempting the same victims or over-preempting additional workloads.

This however introduces the issue, where another workload may now fit because of the Preemptions, where it should be considered a DeferredFit as it will fit only once the Preemptions happen. We need a fix that will ensure native planner accounts for this issue.

**How to reproduce it (as minimally and precisely as possible)**:

1. Enable `TopologyAwareScheduling` and `TASRecomputeAssignmentWithinSchedulingCycle` (with native planner, i.e., `SchedulerLibraryDeepIntegration=false`).
2. Set up a cohort (or ClusterQueue) with two nodes (`node-1` and `node-2`, 4 CPU each):
   - Victim workload `v1` (priority 1) is admitted on `node-2` using 4 CPU.
   - `node-1` has 4 CPU free.
3. Submit two pending workloads scheduled in the same cycle:
   - `w1` (priority 10, requires 4 CPU, needs quota preemption of `v1`): nominated on `node-1` with preemption target `v1`.
   - `w2` (priority 5, requires 4 CPU, has sufficient quota in its CQ): nominated on `node-1` with `Fit` mode.
4. During `processEntry`:
   - `w1` is processed first, adds `v1` to `preemptedWorkloads`, and books 4 CPU on `node-1`.
   - `w2` is processed next: `checkFits` returns `FitsCheckNoTAS` (since `node-1` is now taken by `w1`), triggering `needsTASRecompute`.
   - During `w2`'s recomputation, `v1` is still present on `node-2` in the snapshot, so `w2` cannot place on `node-2` without also targeting `v1` (which then fails with overlapping preemption targets) or failing placement altogether.

**Anything else we need to know?**:

### Comment by [@olekzabl](https://github.com/olekzabl) — 2026-10-30T13:46:40Z

In the native planner, the proposed fix would lead to missed opportunities for `Fit` (and returning `DeferredFit` instead).

Here's how. Suppose that:

- All nodes have 1 CPU allocatable, all Pods request 1 CPU.
- There are 4 nodes (`n1`, `n2`, `n3`, `n4`).
- `V` is currently running on `n1` & `n2`.
- `W1` can preempt `V` and has an affinity against `n1` & `n4`.
  (Consequently, it got assigned to `n2` & `n3`, preempting `V`).
- `W2`, before `updateAssignmentIfNeeded`, was evaluated in isolation from `W1`, and hence got assigned to `n3`.

Now, we do `updateAssignmentIfNeeded` for `W2`, and discover TAS overlap with `W1`.
How should we re-compute the assignment for `W2` now?

* If we exclude `V` from the snapshot (as proposed above), there will be 2 nodes appearing empty: `n1` & `n4`.
   Then, Kueue will be free to pick either for `W2`. (In fact, it'll likely pick `n1`, due to internal ordering).
   But `n1` is still occupied by `V`, so with this choice the best we can do is demote `W2` to `DeferredFit`.

* OTOH, if we keep `V` in the snapshot, we'll clearly see that `n4` is the only node which is free **both** "now" and "soon".
  Thus, even if such a "workload-set-union" may feel "overgrown" (*), there's a purpose for using it: it's good for detecting the "safest" free nodes.

---

(*) Actually I wouldn't use the word "double-counting", as there's no workload counted doubly. The problem is rather squashing together (by taking set-union) two snapshots representing two different timestamps:
 
- "immediate future", when all workloads with `Fit` will come in
- "deferred future", when all planned preemptions will finish and the `DeferredFit` & `Preempt` workloads will come in as well.

For ease of future discussion, @mimowo and I aligned on the term "union snapshot" for this.

---

So actually I'd rather vote for keeping the native planner behavior as is.
Or maybe consider some improvements, but ones that would avoid "missed opportunities for `Fit`".

### Comment by [@olekzabl](https://github.com/olekzabl) — 2026-10-30T13:51:07Z

One improvement that I'd like to propose here is to make sure the _union snapshot_ is never _overbooked_ - by capping its "total  resource usage" on every node (which is just a "resource-type -> amount" mapping) to never exceed the node's capacity.

Such a capping seems to bring no harm for assignment quality, and can save a `Fit` in some scenarios (e.g. when an overbooked amount on one node would unrealistically exhaust total CQ quota, preventing from successful admitting on another node).

I'll come with an example soon.

### Comment by [@olekzabl](https://github.com/olekzabl) — 2026-10-30T14:04:11Z

Example: suppose that:

* There are 2 nodes, each 10 CPU.
* There are 2 CQs, both fully borrowing from a Cohort, total quota = 20 CPU.
* `V` takes 8 CPU and runs on `n1`.
* `A` takes 3 CPU and runs on `n2`.
* `W1` wants 9 CPU, can preempt `V`, and has affinity against `n2`.
  (Consequently, we assign to `n1`, and preempt `V`).
* `W2` wants 2 CPU, and its initial assignment (unaware of `W1`) was to `n1`.

Then:

* `checkFits` discovers a collision between `W1` and `W2` (both won't fit on `n1`).
* The re-assignment for `W2` uses the union snapshot, containing `V`, `A` and `W1`.
  The total quota usage of those is 8 + 3 + 9 = 20, leaving no quota for `W2`.
* However, in reality, `W2` can be well placed on `n2`. Both node capacities and total quota limit are going to be respected at every moment.

Now, the capped union-snapshot would see the usages of 10 CPU on `n1` and 3 CPU on `n2`, total 13.
So it would see the place for `W2`.

### Comment by [@olekzabl](https://github.com/olekzabl) — 2026-10-30T14:18:06Z

Self-correction: this matter is even more delicate, as "total quota usage" depends on which node of the CQ/Cohort hierarchy we focus on, and (IIUC) is currently not determined by summing up per-k8s-node values.

So I think the "capping idea" is not to cap the snapshot per se; rather do quota accounting - for every hierarchy-node - taking account the per-k8s-node summands where each of them is capped to the k8s-node's capacity. Sth like this.
