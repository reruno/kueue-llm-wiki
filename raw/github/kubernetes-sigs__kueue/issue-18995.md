**What would you like to investigate**:

Dropping of the use of minCount for ElasticJob partial scale up. 

I think we don't need it generally, the only downside I see is that we may need to do the interim admission at "count" which is equal to the current minCount.  However, this could probably be fixed by "initialMinCount" field (or annotation for now). 

**Why is this needed**:

- minCount is misleading, because it can be lowered by scale down. I think the real meaning is "initialMinCount".
- This is a source of bugs like fixed here https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/kueue/pull/16742.

### Comment by [@bolubo](https://github.com/bolubo) — 2026-11-20T08:14:10Z

I ran this against a real kube-apiserver (envtest, KubeRay v1.8 CRDs for RayCluster) with `ElasticJobsViaWorkloadSlicesWithPartialReplicaScaleUp` and the quota expressed in pods.

- RayCluster, four workers scaled down to one: the slice becomes `count=1`/`minCount=1`, while the admission keeps the four-worker grant. Asking for four again with two pods of quota leaves the new slice pending.
- Draining the ClusterQueue and resuming with the same two pods admits that pending four-worker slice at one worker.
- Batch Job, same annotations and gates: after the same scale-down, asking for four with two pods of quota admits the new slice at two replicas, with a pending probe for the remainder. The RayCluster run, which also has a head PodSet, stayed pending at the same point, and I could not tell what makes the two differ — if that is intended, I would like to know which rule to read.
- Without the scale-down, both integrations keep `minCount=4` and stay pending at two pods.
