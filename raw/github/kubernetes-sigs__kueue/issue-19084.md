### Comment by [@mimowo](https://github.com/mimowo) — 2026-11-19T16:01:56Z

I asked AI agent to reconstruct the traffic on the node around the failure, and it summarized like that:

| Resource | Active Pod requests | Node allocatable | Unrequested capacity |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| CPU | 1.81 cores | 7.91 cores | **6.10 cores** |
| Memory | 7.71 GiB | 29.20 GiB | **21.49 GiB** |

So, most likely: (a) no OOM for the init container because we have a lot of mem empty, (b) most likely the pod was not rejected by kubelet due to OutOfCpu.

So, I think the most likely scenario is that the node was deleted by karpenter due to node consolidation, after 20min from the previous job end. 

The AI found the [previous job](https://storage.googleapis.com/kubernetes-ci-logs/pr-logs/pull/kubernetes-sigs_kueue/18986/pull-kueue-test-tas-scheduling-perf-release-0-19/2122732214411071488/podinfo.json) finished "16:53:07". Then deletion began 20m27s after that job finished, just as the new Pod landed. The kubelet recorded graceful shutdown at "17:13:41", five seconds after Prow reported failure, and subsequently recorded NodeShutdown rejections.

I'm not yet sure how we could prevent the flakes in that scenarios. The AI suggests using `karpenter.sh/do-not-disrupt: "1h"` for the CI job annotations, but I'm not exactly yet how that would help. I think this path is worth exploring a bit more.
