## Description

**What happened**:

With the partial scale-up strategy (`kueue.x-k8s.io/elastic-job-scale-up-strategy: partial`), an elastic SparkApplication with `minExecutors` below `initialExecutors` can end up suspended while one of its Workload slices holds quota. It happens after an idle scale-down followed by a whole eviction, with no scale-up probe pending at the eviction:

1. The first slice is admitted all-or-nothing with 3 executors (#19250), so its executor `minCount` is 3.
2. The app goes idle and Spark keeps 1 executor. Kueue lowers the slice to count 1 and its `minCount` to 1 (#19249).
3. A higher-priority workload preempts the app as a whole. With no driver, `numExecutors` returns the initial target again (`pkg/controller/jobs/sparkapplication/sparkapplication_controller.go:515`), so the requeued slice asks for 3 executors, while its `minCount` stays 1 (`pkg/workload/podsetscounts.go:134` lowers it and nothing raises it back).
4. With room for the driver + 1 executor, the slice is admitted partially.
5. `EnsureWorkloadSlices` sees an admitted slice below its count and creates a scale-up probe (`pkg/workloadslicing/manager.go:106`), although the job is suspended. `normalizeActiveSlices` returns the pending probe, and `reconcileSuspendedJob` does not start a job whose Workload is not admitted.

The app stays `suspend: true` with no driver while its admitted slice holds the quota of the driver + 1 executor, until the probe fits in full. If it never fits, the quota is held and the app never runs.

The end state is the one in #19046, but no probe has to be pending when the slice is evicted. #19250 does not prevent it, because the slice was admitted before.

**What you expected to happen**:

After a partial re-admission the app starts with what was admitted, and the probe keeps waiting for the rest, as for a running app. Or the re-admission is all-or-nothing, as for a first admission. In both cases a suspended app holds no quota.

**How to reproduce it (as minimally and precisely as possible)**:

1. Enable `SparkApplicationIntegration`, `ElasticJobsViaWorkloadSlices`, `ElasticJobsViaWorkloadSlicesForSparkApplication` and `ElasticJobsViaWorkloadSlicesWithPartialReplicaScaleUp`.
2. A ClusterQueue with 2 CPU and `preemption.withinClusterQueue: LowerPriority`; every pod requests 500m (4 pods fit). WorkloadPriorityClasses `low` (100) and `high` (1000).
3. Create a low-priority elastic SparkApplication with the partial strategy that fills the queue:

```yaml
metadata:
  labels:
    kueue.x-k8s.io/queue-name: spark-lq
    kueue.x-k8s.io/priority-class: low
  annotations:
    kueue.x-k8s.io/elastic-job: "true"
    kueue.x-k8s.io/elastic-job-scale-up-strategy: partial
spec:
  dynamicAllocation:
    enabled: true
    initialExecutors: 3
    minExecutors: 1
    maxExecutors: 3
    shuffleTrackingEnabled: true
  sparkConf:
    spark.dynamicAllocation.executorIdleTimeout: 15s
  driver:
    coreRequest: 500m
  executor:
    instances: 1
    coreRequest: 500m
```

4. Let the app go idle until its slice shows 1 executor (`minCount: 1`) and the ClusterQueue uses 1 CPU.
5. Create a `high` batch/Job with 3 pods of 500m (1.5 CPU). It preempts the app as a whole; the app is suspended and its slice asks for 3 executors with `minCount: 1`.
6. Create a `high` batch/Job with 2 pods of 500m (1 CPU), which stays pending, then delete the 3-pod Job.
7. The 2-pod Job is admitted, and the app's slice is admitted with the driver + 1 executor. A probe for 3 executors stays pending, and the SparkApplication stays `suspend: true` with no driver pod while the ClusterQueue is full (2/2 CPU). It starts only after the 2-pod Job is deleted and the probe fits.

**Anything else we need to know?**:

#19219 (the fix for #19046) fixes this too: with it merged onto main, the app is unsuspended after step 7 and runs with the driver + 1 executor. Its tests cover batch/Job with a probe pending at the eviction, not this path.

The path is specific to SparkApplication: after an eviction its executor count goes back to the initial target, while a batch/Job keeps its scaled-down parallelism.
