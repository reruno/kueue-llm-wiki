#### What this PR does / why we need it:

We have a CQ with a lot of TAS RFs linked to nodes that that may be in unschedulable state. Depending on the RF order in the CQ and the nodes unschedulable state we see significant requeues and scheduling latency since the scheduler tries every flavor in order. The scheduler should not try to use flavors that do not have domains. The check is cheap so I don't think these changes will add performance regressions

### Comment by [@GonzaloLuminary](https://github.com/GonzaloLuminary) — 2026-06-22T15:16:57Z

>could you elaborate what is the user-observable benefit - is it performance or less confusing messages, or something else? Understanding the UX change will help a lot.

This helps to fix scheduling performance issues and log churn that happens in one of our CQs that has 100s of TAS RFs. Many of those TAS sometimes match unschedulable nodes which don't have domains. In that case, kueue loops over all RFs sequentially and fails scheduling because there are no domains. The latter results in unnecessary requeues.

I think we could further improve these checks by including a cheap comparison of the PodSet total amount of resources vs the resources available in the flavor (ignoring topology) to avoid unnecessary requeues. The latter changes are probably a lot more invasive.

### Comment by [@GonzaloLuminary](https://github.com/GonzaloLuminary) — 2026-06-25T22:02:46Z

We have CQs with hundreds of RFs and, as we balance these RFs around at the infra level, many of the RFs stop having domains. Given the scheduler backoff, each requeue due to RFs being empty can take more than 10ms (it was worse in the past with 100ms backoff). We lose a lot of scheduling throughout and log churn due to the scheduler back off. If the flavor assigner knows that the RF doesn't have domains then there's no point for the scheduler to process said flavor.
