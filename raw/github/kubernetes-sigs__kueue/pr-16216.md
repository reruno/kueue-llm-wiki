### Security Architecture Review

**Security architecture risk:** _🟡 Moderate_ · up to `af671`

Matching PodSets by name avoids positional mistakes, but a copied minimum capped at the new size can still be replaced by a larger live grant during scheduling. The effect on partial admission, and whether it is newly reachable, remains unresolved.

**Retained concerns**
- **Medium · security · inferred:** The new controller-side floor cap is not preserved when the scheduler substitutes an admitted predecessor’s live grant. In a mixed shrink-and-growth transition, a grant above the shrunken PodSet’s Count produces a negative reduction delta; whether downstream controls reject the resulting assignment, and whether this PR newly exposes it, is unresolved.

- _inferred_ — The identified exposure concerns admission of replacement Workloads for eligible elastic Jobs. The inspected replacement lookup checks namespace equality; no cross-namespace or privilege expansion is established by this change.

**Trust Boundaries and Controls**
- _observed_ — Workload creation sets the Job as controller owner. At the scheduler boundary, replacement lookup also requires the predecessor to be in the same namespace; neither control bounds a substituted live grant by the replacement PodSet Count.

**Resilience and Maintainability Implications**

**Hardening Proposals**
- _proposed_ — Check the live-grant-versus-replacement-Count invariant where the scheduler substitutes its effective floor, and cover a mixed shrink-and-growth transition through reconciliation and admission rather than helper construction alone.
