#### What this PR does / why we need it?

Remove direct suspension of managed child jobs from the generic job reconciler and leave their lifecycle to the ancestor job's controller.

During MultiKueue workload-slice replacement, the old remote Workload can be finished before the replacement is claimed by the remote parent job. An owner-reference lookup can then find no unfinished Workload and suspend a running child, such as a RayJob's RayCluster, even though the replacement already has admission.

The non-top-level reconciliation path now returns without modifying the child. Remove the two private helpers and import made unused by this change.

Related: https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/kueue/pull/16259

#### Useful notes for your reviewer

This is a draft to evaluate the broader lifecycle change in CI. It applies to all managed child jobs, including when an ancestor Workload is missing or evicted; it relies on parent controllers to stop their children appropriately.

Validation: `go test -mod=mod ./pkg/controller/jobframework` and `make ci-lint` passed. `make verify` was attempted, but its skills lint requires a running Docker daemon, which is unavailable locally.

AI assistance: Codex helped prepare this change and PR description.

### Security Architecture Review

**Security architecture risk:** _🟡 Moderate_ · up to `c3dae`

The change avoids incorrect suspension during workload replacement, but also removes a fallback that stops children without valid ancestor admission. Parent controllers handle normal cleanup, yet user-authored owner metadata and interrupted cleanup can leave children outside that enforcement. The effective exposure depends on enabled integrations, namespace permissions, and additional admission policies.

**Retained concerns**
- **Medium · security · inferred:** Default-on delegation removes the running-child admission fallback without establishing that the ancestor actually lifecycle-manages every child accepted through owner metadata. A principal permitted to create batch Jobs and obtain a managed RayJob's UID could supply a matching controller owner reference on a separately named, unsuspended Job. Existing defaulting exempts that child from initial suspension; the changed reconciler then skips ancestor admission checks, while KubeRay cleanup targets its associated named resources. The base reconciler would suspend this child when the ancestor Workload was missing or unadmitted. Ownership spoofing predates this PR, but the change broadens the states in which it can sustain execution outside ancestor admission. Additional deployment policies may prevent this path.

- _inferred_ — The affected population is managed non-top-level jobs whose ancestor chain resolves in an eligible namespace. **Security Findings and Attack Paths**
- _inferred_ — A separately named batch Job can claim a real managed RayJob as controller owner. Resolved ancestry exempts it from initial suspension, and the new default skips runtime Workload checks. KubeRay's named-resource cleanup does not establish cessation for that arbitrary Job. The preexisting owner-metadata trust limitation is therefore more consequential when ancestor admission is absent. **Trust Boundaries and Controls**

**Resilience and Maintainability Implications**
- _observed_ — KubeRay preserves cleanup ordering once a RayJob enters Suspending, even if spec.suspend is immediately cleared. It retries deletion failures, waits for associated resources to disappear, clears status, and permits restart after reaching Suspended. This is strong representative recovery evidence, not proof of equivalent guarantees for every managed ancestor integration.

**Hardening Proposals**
- _proposed_ — Consider restricting delegation to children with established controller provenance and a proven cessation contract, or narrowing the exception to an authorized replacement handover while retaining fallback enforcement for missing or revoked admission.

  - With `AUTOSCALER_UPDATE_INTERVAL_S` set to 1 second, the autoscaler may observe the two detached actor requests in separate update cycles. As a result, the desired worker replica count may increase from 0 (W0) → 1 (W1) → 2 (W2), rather than directly from 0 → 2.
