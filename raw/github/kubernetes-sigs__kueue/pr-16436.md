### Security Architecture Review

**Security architecture risk:** _🔵 Low_ · up to `19c1c`

The change suspends managed child jobs under conditions previously skipped by default, without adding permissions in the inspected code. Recovery after parent admission or rollback still requires lifecycle handling outside this suspension path, and that handling is not fully established.

**Retained concerns**
No architecture-level concerns identified.

<details>
<summary>Security review details</summary>

**Security Blast Radius**
- _inferred_ — The default change can affect eligible managed children across namespaces handled by the controller. Each inspected reconciliation writes only its selected child, and ancestor lookup remains in that child’s namespace. The deployed population and downstream effects of suspension are not quantified.

**Trust Boundaries and Controls**
- _observed_ — Job metadata and controller references influence ancestor selection. Traversal validates each referenced object’s UID and detects cyclic or excessively deep ownership chains. The subsequent Workload lookup is namespace and owner group/kind/name scoped, without UID filtering in the inspected lookup. These mechanisms predate this PR.

**Resilience and Maintainability Implications**
- _observed_ — The added cases establish initial suspension behavior under both gate values, but do not establish subsequent recovery after admission or reversal of the gate. The inspected generic child branch supplies no unsuspension action, leaving recovery ownership as an unresolved transition guarantee rather than a verified failure.

**Hardening Proposals**
- _proposed_ — Validate the complete transition from child suspension through ancestor admission and gate rollback, identifying the controller responsible for resumption and exercising concurrent ancestor changes and failed child writes. This would establish recovery ownership without treating a missing guarantee as an observed defect.
