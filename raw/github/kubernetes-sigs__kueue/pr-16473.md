## Description

#### What type of PR is this?

/kind bug
/area multikueue

#### What this PR does / why we need it?

When a workload slice is scaled, the scheduler reserves quota for the replacement slice and then finishes the old slice. If that finish fails (for example, because of an update conflict), the old slice stays unfinished and keeps holding quota. The ordinary job reconciliation path recovers from this, but the prebuilt workload path, which MultiKueue worker clusters use, does not.

This PR finds the slices that should have been replaced and finishes them during prebuilt job reconciliation. A slice should have been replaced when a replacement points to it through `replacement-for`, and that replacement holds quota and is neither evicted nor finished.

Related: #16458

#### Useful notes for your reviewer

- Tests: a unit test for `FinishReplacedWorkloadSlices`, a reconcile test, and a MultiKueue integration test that simulates worker-side autoscaling of a RayJob where the scheduler did not finish the replaced slice.
- Also enables `SkipChildJobSuspension` in the MultiKueue Ray autoscaling e2e configuration. The Ray autoscaling e2e job passed 5 out of 5 runs on an earlier commit of this PR.
- This PR was developed with AI assistance.

### Security Architecture Review

**Security architecture risk:** _🔵 Low_ · up to `da98d`

The recovery preserves quota-before-completion ordering and supports retry after failed updates. However, it identifies predecessors through mutable slice metadata without independently checking their ownership or quota domain. Whether this creates an authorization problem depends on deployment permissions; no reachable privilege escalation was established.

**Retained concerns**
- **Medium · security · inferred:** The new recovery path treats same-namespace slice-name and replacement-for metadata, plus replacement quota reservation, as sufficient authority to finish a predecessor. It does not independently bind that predecessor to the reconciled job or the replacement's ClusterQueue. If a principal can author qualifying slice metadata without authority over the referenced predecessor, controller status-write authority could cross a job or quota-domain boundary. This is a conditional concern, not a verified attack: ordinary producers establish job-derived relationships, and shipped workload-editor permissions already allow workload deletion. Deployment-specific reachability remains unresolved.

<details>
<summary>Security review details</summary>

**Security Blast Radius**
- _inferred_ — A spoofed qualifying relationship would be confined by the helper's namespace filter, but its selection is not bounded by owner, ClusterQueue, or predecessor elasticity. Finishing affects scheduler queue and quota-cache accounting, so a mistaken relationship can have consequences beyond metadata bookkeeping.

**Security Findings and Attack Paths**
- _inferred_ — The conditional attack path is authored slice metadata plus legitimate replacement quota reservation, followed by enabled prebuilt reconciliation and a controller-authorized Finished status write to the referenced workload. No deployment principal with those inputs but without equivalent authority over the target was established; this remains an authorization proof gap rather than a verified exploit.

**Trust Boundaries and Controls**
- _observed_ — Ordinary slice generation derives replacement-for from job-indexed existing workloads and sets the chain name itself. Generic job annotation copying is filtered or configuration-selected rather than unrestricted. These controls counter a simple job-annotation-only spoofing claim.
- _observed_ — The inspected Workload validators do not bind slice-name or replacement-for annotations to an owner or ClusterQueue. The shipped workload-editor role grants workload deletion and metadata mutation but only read access to workload status; its broad deletion authority is important counterevidence against treating controller completion as a demonstrated privilege gain.

**Resilience and Maintainability Implications**
- _observed_ — Recovery follows the scheduler's reserve-before-finish handoff and excludes pending, evicted, or finished replacements. Error propagation and finished-condition guards provide partial-failure containment, although the helper does not make replacement eligibility and predecessor completion one atomic transition.

**Hardening Proposals**
- _proposed_ — Consider binding recovery to validated chain provenance and quota-domain identity appropriate to MultiKueue handover, rather than relying solely on mutable annotations. Establish the permissions and admission policy for direct Workload writers before treating those annotations as an authorization boundary.
