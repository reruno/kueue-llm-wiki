#### What this PR does / why we need it?

`status.fairSharing.weightedShare` is an int64, and `core.WeightedShare` filled it from the cache's float64 share with `int64(math.Ceil(f))`, special-casing only `+Inf`. A finite share at or past 2^63 has no int64 value, the Go spec leaves the result of that conversion implementation-dependent, and on amd64 it is `math.MinInt64`. A ClusterQueue with weight `2n` that borrows 30000 CPU while its Cohort lends `1m` has a share of 1.5e19, so on amd64 its status read `-9223372036854775808`.

This adds `SaturatingCeil` to `pkg/util/math`, beside the other saturating helpers, and uses it in `core.WeightedShare`, so such a share is reported as `math.MaxInt64` like `+Inf` already was. `roundedWeightedShare`, which only the cache's unit tests call, uses it too. Scheduling and preemption compare the float64 share through `CompareDRS` and do not change.

Part of #16477.

#### Useful notes for your reviewer

`float64(math.MaxInt64)` rounds to 2^63, one past the int64 range, which is why the check is `>=`.

The `roundedWeightedShare` comment also stops saying that a zero share means the usage is below the nominal quota, since a node borrowing only resources the Cohort has nothing lendable for can get zero as well (#16529).

The integration test admits a workload within the nominal quota of a weight-`2n` ClusterQueue, withdraws that quota while the workload stays admitted, and reads the status: on amd64 it is `-9223372036854775808` on main and `9223372036854775807` with this change. `TestDominantResourceShare` covers the ClusterQueue and Cohort shares through `roundedWeightedShare`, and `TestSaturatingCeil` the boundaries.

### Comment by [@thc1006](https://github.com/thc1006) — 2026-10-02T02:21:05Z

> Out of curiosity - are the computations (before casting to int64) more or less correct with these odd values?

Yes. The amounts are exact integers and `PerThousandOf` rounds only its result, so 30000 CPU borrowed against 1m lendable gives a ratio of 3e10, and dividing by the 2n weight in float64 gives 1.5e19, the same as the exact value. That just doesn't fit an int64, and the old `int64(math.Ceil(f))` turned it into -9223372036854775808 on amd64. This PR only changes that conversion into the status. `CompareDRS` still orders the float64 shares, so with 60000 CPU borrowed (a share of 3e19) the two queues still compare as different although both report MaxInt64. At this size the shares aren't exact either, since float64 is only precise to 2048 around 1.5e19.
