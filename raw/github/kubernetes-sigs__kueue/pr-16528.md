#### What this PR does / why we need it?

`PreciseWeightedShare` divides the unweighted ratio by the weight in float64. A ClusterQueue with weight `1e308` that borrows 1m of CPU against 9e15 lendable cores has a ratio of 1000 / 9e18, about 1.1e-16, and a share of about 1.1e-324, which float64 rounds to 0. A weight past the float64 range, such as `1e400`, becomes `+Inf` as a float64, and any finite ratio divided by it is 0. Such a node then had the share of a node within its quota: its status reported `weightedShare: 0`, and `CompareDRS` tied it with a node that does not borrow.

`PreciseWeightedShare` now returns `math.SmallestNonzeroFloat64` when a positive ratio divides down to zero, so the status reports 1. Earlier revisions of #15026 carried this floor before it was split out, and on #16477 it was suggested as its own PR.

Part of #16477.

#### Useful notes for your reviewer

The floor keeps such a share above zero, not the order or the magnitude that float64 has already lost. Two floored shares compare equal, and a weight past the float64 range is `+Inf` before the division even when the exact share is representable: a ratio of 1000 over a weight of `1e309` is 1e-306, yet it is floored to 5e-324, below the 1e-315 that a ratio of 1e-7 over a weight of `1e308` gets. That order was already wrong before this PR, with 0 in place of 5e-324. The weighted share metrics show the float64 share, so for such a node they read 5e-324 instead of 0, and the V(4) scheduler logs spell it out as a 326-character decimal.

The ratio itself does not reach zero: `PerThousandOf` divides exact amounts, and the lendable side is a sum of quotas each capped at the largest Quantity, so a positive ratio stays far above the smallest float64.

The integration test admits a 1m CPU workload to a weight-`1e308` ClusterQueue that has no nominal quota, so it borrows from the Cohort, and reads the status: `0` on main, `1` with this change. `TestDominantResourceShare` covers a ClusterQueue and a Cohort with weight `1e308`, and a ClusterQueue with weight `1e400`. Those checks read the share rounded up to an integer, which a floor of 1 would also pass, so `TestPreciseWeightedShare` checks the float64 share itself, and `TestCompareDRS` its order against a node that does not borrow, a small share float64 holds and another floored share, in both argument orders.

This is rebased on #16488, whose zero-weight check stays ahead of the zero-ratio return. #16487 adds cases to the same test table, so whichever of the two merges later needs a small rebase.
