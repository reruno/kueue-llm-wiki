  1. [sched-lib#65](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/scheduler-library/pull/65) fixes a sched-lib bug.
     Before that fix, sched-lib treats _everything_ as an "all-or-nothing gang", regardless of actual [Composite]PodGroup's `.SchedulingPolicy`, or of the `GangScheduling` plugin status.
     That's why enabling this plugin right now has no observable effect (yet).
  
  2. ... Well, it has one observable effect: sched-lib should stop emitting the following logs:
     `GenericWorkload is enabled, but GangScheduling plugin is not set. Gang scheduling using PodGroups may not work as expected.`
     (Which is what brought us originally to this topic).
     But (for now) it's just logging, not actual behavior.
  
  3. After [#65](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/scheduler-library/pull/65), sched-lib will:
     - With `GangScheduling` enabled - handle `SchedulingPolicy.Gang` (i.e. specs saying "this PodGroup is a gang") correctly.
     - With `GangScheduling` disabled - ignore `SchedulingPolicy`, that is:
       - be happy with scheduling just 1 Pod
       - try to schedule more only if it isn't problematic (e.g. requires no _additional_ preemptions)
  
  4. Even without `GangScheduling`, Kueue logic will still enforce gang-admission.
     So there's no risk of partial admission.
  
     However, there's a large risk of Kueue **blocking admission** for a Workload which was actually admissible - just because sched-lib happily returned a placement for only a subset of its Pods.
  
     This is what we expect with [#65](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/scheduler-library/pull/65) but without this PR.
  
     Examples:
     - Preemptions:
       Given 2 nodes `n1, n2`, with `n2` occupied by a Workload `low-prio`, and a newly coming 2-Pod Workload `high-prio`,
       sched-lib will be free to say "place 1 Pod on `n1` and that's enough".
       In that case, Kueue will block admission, even though the second Pod could preempt `low-prio`.
     - TAS requirements:
       Given 2 blocks of nodes `[n1, n2], [n3, n4]`, with `n4` occupied, and a 2-Pod Workload requesting "same block",
       sched-lib will be free to say "place 2 Pods on `n1, n2`", or "place 1 Pod on `n3` and that's enough".
       In the latter case, Kueue will block admission, even though the Workload is actually admissible because `n1, n2` is a valid gang-compliant choice.
