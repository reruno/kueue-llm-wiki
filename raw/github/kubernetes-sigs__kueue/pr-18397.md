## Description

#### What type of PR is this?

#### What this PR does / why we need it?

During an elastic scale-up, the scheduler cache charges the ClusterQueue for both the replacement workload slice and the slice it replaces, from the moment the replacement is assumed until the replaced slice's `Finished` update arrives. In that window the ClusterQueue's usage exceeds what the job runs and can exceed its quota, and the scheduler admits and preempts against the inflated usage.

With this change the cache charges each chain of workload slices once: a slice covered by another slice of its chain is not charged. A chain is identified by the namespace, the controller and the `kueue.x-k8s.io/workload-slice-name` annotation, which every slice of a chain carries, and a `workloadSliceTracker` keeps its slices. The larger of two slices covers the smaller one, and the newer one covers the older one when they charge the same, so a replacement that shrinks while it waits for quota stays covered by the slice it replaces until that slice finishes. Covered slices leave `Workloads`, so the ClusterQueue and LocalQueue usage, the workload counts and the scheduler snapshot all exclude them, and they are no longer preemption candidates. When the covering slice leaves the cache first, for example when its admission fails or it is evicted, the remaining slices are charged again.

Fixes #18396

- **Ordering.** Slices with the same usage are ordered by creation time. Slices with the same usage created in the same second, and slices that each charge more of some resource, all stay charged, so a tie can over-charge but never under-charge; a unit case covers it.
- **Shrinking replacements.** A replacement can shrink while it waits for quota, for example when a Spark driver lowers its executor target. The larger slice then covers the smaller one until it finishes, so a failed admission of the smaller one cannot leave the ClusterQueue over quota.
- **TAS.** TAS slices keep the previous accounting: a newer slice may be placed on other nodes than the running Pods of an older one.
- **Identity.** The job UID label is not used, since `ConstructWorkload` omits it when the UID is not a valid label value. The `TestSchedule` slices have no controller or slice name, so they keep the previous accounting and their expectations are unchanged.
- **`make verify`** could not run on my machine: it requires GNU sed, and its tool installer cannot download the pinned tools from this network. `gofmt` and `go vet` on the changed packages are clean.
