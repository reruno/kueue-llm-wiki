Add support for in-cycle & cross-cycle committed victims, on par with [sched-lib#70](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/scheduler-library/pull/70).

**Heavily based** on @Singularity23x0 's #17740, #18546 and #18297.
(Essentially, vibe-coded starting with "get those 3 and try addressing comments on them". With a few rounds of later refinements). 

This is **AI slop** for now, to be revised & polished before un-drafting.

If you want to review this, **review single commits**, this should be easier.

Key differences comparing to @Singularity23x0 's original PRs:

- First consume [sched-lib#70](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/scheduler-library/pull/70), then [sched-lib#75](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/scheduler-library/pull/75), then do stuff.
  That is: don't support the old preemptions model; don't face problems with pod-less victims.
- Remove committed victims from the WAS snapshot early and forever.
  (Thanks to this, avoid `.Simulate()` and WAS transactions).
- OTOH, in the Kueue snapshot, keep committed victims essentially same way as non-WAS path does.
  (In particular: let them be seen by `FlavorAssigner`, `PreemptionOracle`, and "immediate fit" checks).
- When deciding which Workloads / Pods are committed victims:
  - For Workloads: keep using `preemptionExpectations` but generalize from `EvictedByPreemption` to all `Evicted` Workloads.
  - For Pods: replace simplistic check `.DeletionTimestamp != nil` with relying on the recently-added "residual Pods" feature (for now, restricting there to terminating Pods, just out of conservatism).
- Don't even start `BareManagerSnapshot` etc.
- Fix an issue (introduced in the original PRs) that any `DeferredFit` workload could become sticky (under BestEffortFIFO) and thus undesiredly block higher-priority peers from being admitted.

Let me check with you how concerning it is now (after the recent changes):

- The current structure of plumbing CVs into Kueue & WAS snapshots:
  - "Permanent" mutations (until the end of scheduling cycle):
    - When taking the snapshot: `.AddCommittedVictims` + `.AddTerminatingPodsAsCommittedVictims` (acting on WAS snapshot).
    - In the processing phase: `.AddCommittedVictims` again.
      (To add the Workload victims of the just-accepted Workload. Residual Pods can't appear at this point).
  - Temporary mutations:
    - In `updateAssignmentIfNeeded` -> `checkFits` -> `fits`: `.SimulateWorkloadUsageRemoval` + `.SimulatePodUsageRemoval` (acting on Kueue snapshot).
    - In `updateAssignmentIfNeeded` -> around `getAssignments`, in "target overlap" case: `simulateOtherPreemptions` (acting on both snapshots, except for WAS snapshot for WAS deep-int, already stripped of CVs "permanently").
    - In the WAS planner: `.SimulateCommittedVictimsRemoval` (acting on Kueue snapshot).
      (Residual Pods require no treatment here - they don't hold Kueue quota, and their TAS usage in the Kueue snapshot is not considered).

- Given our current choice of logic, I'm feeling the above structure is now reasonably clear.
  Workload CVs and Pod CVs are handled by different calls, but close to each other.
  (The separation is mostly for cleaner typing).

- As for combining different runners on different flavors:
  - As we recently decided to postpone this to a later future, if not indefinitely, it's becoming less essential to consider it now.
  - Also, with the current structure, I don't see how WAS deep-int & non-WAS paths could interfere badly in CV management.
    Basically, the mutations listed above either are invisible to non-WAS path (as they only touch WAS snapshot) or seem applicable to it (the "smarter" CV treatment in `updateAssignmentIfNeeded`).
    While the latter case could produce subtle semantic divergences wrt. Kueue main, even for non-WAS, this feels acceptable behind the WAS deep-int FG.
  - One potential issue, indeed, is interference between WAS deep-int for some Workloads / PodSets and WAS basic-int for others, assuming they share the same WAS snapshot.
    It's not clear if support for this will be needed.
    Even if it will, this seems resolvable, at the very least, by using 2 WAS snapshots (one for basic-int, one for deep-int). Or we may try optimizing this.

- What did you mean by "3 feature flags"? I see just `SchedulerLibraryDeepIntegration`.

  Thank you for asking. TL;DR: This is a heavy topic, and I _partially_ agree with you.
  
  1. The _main goal_ of this effort for now is to ensure determinism of victim selection for a single preemptor across scheduling cycles.
     (As otherwise next cycles would "randomly" preempt more victims, undesiredly).
     Of course, assuming no _significant changes around_ in the meantime.
  
     This main goal is achieved here, in the same as in the current Kueue code.
     If X could preempt Y once, it will be able to preempt it consistently. (Well, not strictly _always_, but exceptions count as _significant changes around_).
     Hence, even if re-assignment for X still sees Y, the initial mode should be `Preempt`. Not `NoFit`.
  
  2. Of course, it would be sad to achieve just the _main goal_ and miss the opportunity for other desired gains.
     I'm feeling your point: as we're already gaining from DeferredFit in some cases, why not apply that consistently everywhere?
     This was indeed my starting point when working on this PR.
     However, I learnt this would bring some more complications.
  
     * The main complication, AFAIK, is about flavor assignment.
       Suppose a Workload X has a choice between a F1 (where it'd get `DeferredFit`) or F2 (where it'd get `Fit`). In that order.
  
       - On Kueue main: We consider F1 -> `Preempt`. Assuming certain FlavorFungibility settings, we then consider F2 -> `Fit`. We choose F2.
       - On this PR: Same as above.
       - On this PR + your suggestion: We consider F1 -> `Fit`. We choose F1, regardless of FlavorFungibility. X waits on F1 while F2 would admit it right away.
     
       This feels like a degradation, even if we restrict this just to the WAS deep-int case.
  
       This feels ultimately fixable, as we discussed today. We could have two rounds of flavor assignments, one with CVs and one without. Or, we could teach FlavorAssigner about committed victims, make it sometimes return `DeferredFit` mode, possibly expand FlavorFungibility with `WhenCanWait`, etc.
       But these are all substantial core changes in additional layers of code, which I'd prefer to carefully consider in a follow-up.
  
     * I'm sensing other potential complications as well (which I'd like to double-think before proceeding) - for example:
  
       - Changing DRS values used in the tournament when generating preemption candidates.
         This feels right but I'm constantly anxious of hidden traps like #14543 .
         (Well, we may have such traps anyway - but I'm feeling the current solution leaves us more room for maneuvering).
  
       - Feasibility of #18889.
         (Also not yet well understood but intuitively easier when the Kueue snapshot is less affected by CV removal).
  
  3. As a result, I'm trying to be possibly _conservative_ here.
    That is: win our _main goal_, avoid degradations, and besides that - follow the current Kueue behavior as much as possible.
    Yes, it's not optimal in the long term.
    I'll be happy to work on follow-ups.
  
  4. And finally, I admit you still had a point besides the above discussion.
     My originally proposed code introduced a degradation in one specific case: [preemption targets overlap](https://github.com/kubernetes-sigs/kueue/blob/9a9faff281837ca710747063068a7d5b6bcb0f3f/pkg/scheduler/scheduler.go#L986-L995).
  
     In this case, old Kueue logic _would remove_ CVs (well, just in-cycle ones) from the Kueue snapshot, while this code wouldn't.
     And this can indeed degrade behavior, though under quite complex assumptions.
     The simplest story I can imagine is roughly this:
     * A Workload X in cq1 wants to preempt V1 and V2 (though only partially consuming the space after either of those).
     * Then, Y in cq2 comes. Without X, it would fit by preempting V1. OTOH with X added, Y also needs the space freed by V2 (and not taken by X).
     * Y can't preempt V2, e.g. due to priority.
     * And moreover, Y perhaps should be borrowing (so that insufficient candidates lead to `NoFit` on the re-assignment for Y, rather than `Preempt` with no candidates).
  
     Feels an edge case but a valid one.
  
     So I just changed this PR to handle that (by removing CVs from Kueue snapshot on overlap recompute, just like old Kueue does).
